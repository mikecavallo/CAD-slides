CH03,S01: The ABCs of Behavior
On-screen text: The ABCs of Behavior / Coming up / What is happening around the behavior? / A B C
Narration:
At the end of the last chapter, we said we were going to add temperature to our pot.
Before we do that, we need one more piece of the framework.
We need a way to look at what is happening around the behavior.
That's where the ABCs come in.

CH03,S02: The ABCs
On-screen text: A = Antecedent / The thing + the context / B = Behavior / Emotional response / C = Consequence / An Excited Greeting
Narration:
A is the antecedent, the thing that happens and the context around it.
The immediate thing matters, but so does where it happens and what that context has come to predict for the dog.
B is the behavior, what your dog does, for example barking or lunging.
Between A and B, the dog has an emotional response. We don't always know exactly what the dog is feeling, but something is happening inside the dog before we see the behavior.
C is the consequence, what happens as a direct result of the behavior.
Let's put an example into it. Here's an excited greeting.
A person appears outside the window.
The dog has an emotional response, jumps at the window, and barks.
Then the person comes inside and gives the dog attention.

CH03,S03: WTF? What's the Function?
On-screen text: WTF? What's the Function? / What did the behavior accomplish? / Possible function: decrease distance / gain attention
Narration:
Once we have the ABC, we can ask an important question: WTF? What's the function?
In other words, what did the behavior accomplish?
In the excited greeting, the barking may be helping the dog get closer to the person or gain attention.
Let's look at two more examples.

CH03,S04: A Person Approaches
On-screen text: A Person Approaches / Possible function: increase distance
Narration:
Now let's change the situation.
You're out on a walk and a person heads toward your dog.
Your dog stops and barks.
The person stops or moves away.
This time, the barking may have helped create more distance.

CH03,S05: Guarding a Toy
On-screen text: Guarding a Toy / Possible function: maintain access
Narration:
Now imagine a dog has a toy and another dog approaches.
The dog stiffens, barks, growls, and may snap.
The other dog goes away, and the dog keeps the toy.
Here, the behavior may be helping the dog keep access to something valuable.

CH03,S06: Same Behavior. Different Job.
On-screen text: Same Behavior. Different Job. / Bark. Bark. Bark. / Same behavior. Different outcome. Different possible function. / Look at the whole picture with the ABCs, not just the behavior / It worked, so it's more likely to be repeated.
Narration:
Now notice something important.
All three dogs barked.
But the barking didn't accomplish the same thing.
Same behavior. Different outcome. Different possible function.
That's why we need to look at the whole picture with the ABCs, not just the behavior.
In all three, the barking worked. And when behavior works, it's more likely to be repeated.

CH03,S07: Movement Isn't Function
On-screen text: Movement Isn't Function / Direction is not function / Moved toward / May look like: wants to get closer / In reality: wants the other dog to go away / Result: more distance / Moved toward. Accomplished more space. / Look at the entire picture: the ABCs
Narration:
There's an important catch.
The direction a dog moves doesn't necessarily tell us the function.
A dog may lunge toward another dog, which can make it look like they want to get closer.
But in reality, the dog wants the other dog to go away. And when the other dog moves away, the result is more distance.
The dog moved toward, but the behavior accomplished more space.
That's why we look at the entire picture with the ABCs.

CH03,S08: Why the Pattern Repeats
On-screen text: Why the Pattern Repeats / It worked / The dog got what it wanted or needed out of the situation. / Next time: more likely to happen again
Narration:
So why does a pattern repeat? Because it worked.
The dog got what it wanted or needed out of the situation.
So the next time a similar situation happens, the dog is more likely to do it again.

CH03,S09: Before Training
On-screen text: Before Training / Another dog appears too close. / Emotions / e.g. barking, lunging, growling / Space is given. It worked: the dog got the space it needed, so this is more likely next time.
Narration:
Let's look at an example of what this can look like before training.
Another dog appears. That's the A.
The dog has an emotional response, and we see the B: barking and lunging.
Then space is given. That's the C. The dog got the space it needed, so barking and lunging is more likely to happen again.

CH03,S10: What Training Includes
On-screen text: What Training Includes / Management / Changing emotions / New behaviors
Narration:
So how do we change this pattern? That's what training is for, and training includes three things.
First, management. Management keeps the dog out of the situations that bring up those big emotions, so the old pattern isn't practiced while we work.
Second, changing the dog's emotional response.
And third, teaching new behaviors, so the dog has something else they can do instead, for example looking back at you or checking in with you.

CH03,S11: Building a New Path
On-screen text: Old path: e.g. barking, lunging / New path: e.g. looks back at you, checks in / Management / Back to the old path
Narration:
Remember the path across the lawn from the first chapter? The old path is worn in because the dog has used it so many times.
Training builds a new path. With practice, the new path becomes stronger.
At the same time, the old path begins to fade. That's not only because the old behavior is practiced less. Management keeps the dog out of the situations that bring up those old emotions, so the grass has a chance to grow back.
But that old path hasn't vanished.
Even practicing the old behavior once in a while, when it still works, can make that path grow back. And the dog may automatically fall back on the old path instead of the new one.

CH03,S12: Training in Action
On-screen text: Management, e.g. more distance / Changing emotions / The consequence still needs to work / Same consequence. A better behavior. / After training / Emotions changed / The consequence can change / Building a relationship
Narration:
Here's what that looks like. During training, another dog may still appear. Management might mean, for example, getting more distance from that dog.
But now we're working on changing the dog's emotional response and teaching a new behavior, such as looking back at you.
The consequence still needs to work for the dog. The dog still needs to get what it needs out of the situation, so if they need space, we still give them space.
Now the new behavior leads to the outcome the dog needs, so that new pattern gets practiced instead. The consequence is the same, but the old behavior you wanted to change is gone, and a better behavior has taken its place.
After training, there's less management, because the dog's skills have improved, the emotions have changed, and the new behavior is in place.
And as the dog's emotional response changes, what they need from the consequence can change too.
Your dog might even start wanting something else, like the chance to build a relationship with another dog.

CH03,S13: Your Homework
On-screen text: Your Homework / What was the thing? e.g. Another dog appears / What was the context? e.g. Same corner, dogs too close / What did your dog do? e.g. Barked and lunged / What happened as a result? e.g. The other dog moved away / WTF: What's The Function? e.g. More distance? / It doesn't have to be perfect. I'll help you with that. / Find the thing. Notice the context. See the pattern.
Narration:
Before we move on, I want you to start looking for your dog's ABCs.
Start by finding the thing. What happened before the behavior?
Then look at the context. Where were you? How close was the thing? What was happening around your dog? Is this a place where similar things happen often?
Then write down what your dog did.
Next, look at what happened as a result. Did someone move away? Did your dog get closer? Did they keep something? Did they get attention?
Finally, ask: WTF? What's the function? What did the behavior accomplish?
You don't have to get your dog's ABCs exactly right. I'll help you with that.
The goal is to start finding the thing, noticing the context, and seeing the pattern.
